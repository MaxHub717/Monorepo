NGL Season Lifecycle & Competition Timing Model

**Status:** Canonical
**Version:** v1.0
**Applies to:** NGL Competition & Scheduling Engine v2
**Related:** NGL Competition & Scheduling Specification v2

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the temporal structure of an NGL season.

It establishes:

* how long a season lasts;
* how Season start and end dates are configured;
* how Phase lifetimes are determined;
* how the competition engine fits all Phases inside the Season lifetime;
* how the final Phase is protected from extending beyond the Season;
* which days and hours the scheduling engine may use;
* how the system prevents unrealistic schedules;
* how Season timing affects Phase generation and scheduling.

The central rule is:

> **A Season has a fixed lifetime. All competition Phases must fit within that lifetime, and the final Phase must be completed within the final week of the Season.**

---

# 2. Core Temporal Hierarchy

The competition timing hierarchy is:

```text
Season
│
├── Start Date
├── End Date
│
├── Phase 1
│   ├── Plot
│   └── Series
│
├── Phase 2
│   ├── Plot
│   └── Series
│
├── Phase 3
│
├── ...
│
└── Final Phase
    └── Must conclude within final Season week
```

The Season is the **hard temporal boundary**.

Phases are subordinate to the Season.

A Phase cannot independently extend the Season.

---

# 3. Season Start and End Dates

Every Season must have:

```text
season_start_date
season_end_date
```

These are required when an Administrator creates a Season.

Example:

```text
Season:
2027 Season 1

Start:
1 January 2027

End:
31 March 2027
```

This represents approximately a three-month / twelve-week competition lifetime.

The Season dates become the authoritative time boundary for:

* registration;
* competition phases;
* Series scheduling;
* final competition;
* Season completion.

---

# 4. Season Duration

Season duration is calculated from the configured start and end dates.

The system must not assume that every Season is exactly three months.

Examples:

```text
4-week Season
8-week Season
12-week Season
16-week Season
```

are all technically possible if permitted by the Season configuration.

The competition engine must calculate the actual available scheduling period from the configured dates.

---

# 5. Season Creation Requirements

When creating a Season, the Administrator must provide:

```text
Season Name
Start Date
End Date
Registration Opening
Registration Closing
Competition Configuration
Ruleset
Timezone
```

At minimum, the system must enforce:

```text
Start Date < End Date
```

Registration dates must fit inside the Season lifecycle.

The competition itself must also fit inside the Season lifecycle.

---

# 6. Season Lifetime Is a Hard Constraint

The competition engine must never generate a competition schedule that requires activity beyond:

```text
Season End Date
```

Therefore:

```text
Phase 1
+
Phase 2
+
Phase 3
+
...
+
Final Phase
```

must fit inside the Season lifetime.

The scheduler must reject a schedule if completing the required Series would extend beyond the Season boundary.

---

# 7. Phase Lifetime

A Phase does not have an arbitrary fixed duration.

Its required duration is calculated from:

```text
Number of competing players
+
Number of Series
+
Available scheduling capacity
+
Allowed scheduling days
+
Allowed scheduling hours
+
Series duration
```

Therefore Phase duration is dynamic.

The existing competition model already establishes that Phase duration depends on the number of competitors and that Phase 1 should normally take the longest because it contains the largest field.

---

# 8. Phase Duration Principle

The general rule is:

> **The larger the active competition field, the more scheduling capacity the Phase requires.**

Therefore:

```text
Phase 1
→ largest field
→ largest number of Series
→ longest expected duration
```

As players are eliminated:

```text
Phase 2
→ fewer players
→ fewer Series
→ shorter expected duration
```

Then:

```text
Phase 3
→ fewer players
→ fewer Series
→ shorter expected duration
```

and so on.

The system should therefore naturally compress the later competition stages.

---

# 9. Phase Scheduling Must Be Forward-Looking

The system must not schedule a Phase simply because the Phase itself can fit into the immediately available days.

Before generating or locking a Phase schedule, the system must consider:

```text
Current Phase
+
Remaining Phases
+
Final Phase deadline
```

This prevents Phase 1 from consuming so much time that the later phases cannot realistically be completed.

---

# 10. Final Phase Deadline

The final Phase has a special requirement.

> **The final Phase must be completed within the final week of the Season.**

This creates a protected closing period for the competition.

For example:

```text
Season
12 weeks
│
├── Week 1
├── Week 2
├── Week 3
├── Week 4
├── Week 5
├── Week 6
├── Week 7
├── Week 8
├── Week 9
├── Week 10
├── Week 11
└── Week 12
     ↑
     Final Phase
```

The system must reserve the final week for the final competition stage.

---

# 11. Final Week Is a Protected Competition Window

The final week is not simply leftover time.

It is a protected deadline window.

The competition engine should therefore calculate:

```text
Season End
    ↓
Final Week Boundary
    ↓
Final Phase Deadline
```

The final Phase must be scheduled so that its required Series and result-processing windows complete before the Season ends.

This protects the Season from remaining permanently `ACTIVE` because earlier phases consumed too much time.

---

# 12. Phase Planning Example — 12-Week Season

Example:

```text
Season Duration:
12 weeks
```

Possible competition allocation:

```text
Phase 1
Weeks 1–6

Phase 2
Weeks 7–8

Phase 3
Weeks 9–10

Phase 4
Weeks 11–12
```

This is an **illustrative allocation**, not a hardcoded formula.

The actual system determines the required duration from the number of Series and available scheduling capacity.

The important constraint is:

```text
Final Phase
≤ final Season week
```

---

# 13. Phase 1 Must Not Consume the Entire Season

A common failure would be:

```text
Phase 1
Weeks 1–10

Phase 2
Weeks 11–12
```

when the competition requires:

```text
Phase 2
+
Phase 3
+
Final Phase
```

The scheduler must identify this before Phase 1 is locked.

Therefore Phase generation and scheduling must be **season-aware**.

---

# 14. Remaining Competition Capacity

At any point, the system should be able to determine:

```text
Season Time Remaining
```

and:

```text
Competition Work Remaining
```

Conceptually:

```text
Available Competition Time
        ≥
Required Time For Remaining Phases
```

If the remaining time is insufficient, the system must not silently create an impossible schedule.

It should return a scheduling failure/blocker indicating that the remaining Season lifetime cannot accommodate the required competition.

---

# 15. Scheduling Days

For the current NGL implementation, the allowed competition schedule days are:

```text
Monday
Tuesday
Wednesday
Thursday
Friday
Saturday
```

Sunday is not an allowed Series scheduling day.

Therefore:

```text
Sunday
→ No automatically generated Series
```

This rule applies to the scheduling engine.

---

# 16. Allowed Scheduling Hours

Automatically generated Series may be scheduled only between:

```text
10:00 AM
and
10:00 PM
```

in the applicable competition timezone.

Therefore the scheduler must not create Series appointments outside:

```text
10:00 – 22:00
```

---

# 17. Series Duration Constraint

The Series remains the scheduling unit.

Each Series has a:

```text
1-hour Match Window
```

All three Games belonging to the Series must be played inside that one-hour window.

The existing competition model explicitly defines this requirement.

Therefore the scheduler must reserve a complete one-hour appointment for every Series.

---

# 18. Check-In Window

For a Series beginning at:

```text
T
```

the check-in lifecycle is:

```text
T - 60 minutes
    Check-in opens

T - 30 minutes
    Check-in closes

T
    Series begins
```

This means a player must complete check-in during the one-hour pre-match period.

---

# 19. Match Window

The Series Match Window is:

```text
T → T + 60 minutes
```

All three Games must be completed during this period.

The system must therefore not schedule:

```text
Game 1
10:00

Game 2
11:00

Game 3
12:00
```

as separate appointments.

Instead:

```text
Series
10:00–11:00

Game 1
Game 2
Game 3
```

all occur inside that Series window.

---

# 20. Results Window

After the one-hour Match Window:

```text
T + 60 minutes
```

the match window closes.

The Results Window remains open for an additional:

```text
30 minutes
```

Therefore:

```text
T + 90 minutes
```

is the final results-processing deadline.

Example:

```text
09:00
Check-in opens

09:30
Check-in closes

10:00
Series begins

11:00
Series Match Window closes

11:30
Results Window closes
```

The existing competition source establishes this same relationship between the one-hour match window and the additional 30-minute results window.

---

# 21. Scheduling Time Must Include the Full Series Lifecycle

The scheduler must not calculate capacity using only the one-hour match window.

Each appointment consumes:

```text
Check-in lifecycle
+
1-hour Match Window
+
Results processing deadline
```

However, the **10:00–22:00 scheduling restriction applies to the Series Match Window**.

The system must ensure that the generated appointment does not create an invalid operational window around the allowed schedule.

---

# 22. Earliest Automatically Generated Series

The earliest Series Match Window is:

```text
10:00
```

Therefore:

```text
Check-in opens:
09:00

Check-in closes:
09:30

Match:
10:00–11:00

Results deadline:
11:30
```

This is valid.

---

# 23. Latest Automatically Generated Series

The latest Series Match Window is:

```text
21:00–22:00
```

This ensures that the one-hour Match Window remains completely inside the:

```text
10:00–22:00
```

operating period.

The resulting lifecycle is:

```text
20:00
Check-in opens

20:30
Check-in closes

21:00
Series begins

22:00
Match Window closes

22:30
Results Window closes
```

The Series itself remains inside the allowed scheduling hours.

---

# 24. Timezone

The scheduling hours are interpreted according to the configured competition timezone.

For example:

```text
Competition Timezone:
Africa/Lagos
```

means:

```text
Monday–Saturday
10:00–22:00
Africa/Lagos
```

The stored appointment should retain its timezone context.

The system must not interpret:

```text
10:00
```

as a universal UTC value.

---

# 25. Timezone Rule

The canonical rule is:

> **Allowed scheduling hours are local competition hours, not raw UTC hours.**

The scheduler converts the configured competition-local schedule into its persisted timestamp representation.

This prevents timezone conversion from accidentally moving Series outside the permitted operating period.

---

# 26. Phase Duration Calculation

The scheduling engine should calculate the minimum required Phase duration from:

```text
Series Count
×
Series Scheduling Capacity
```

subject to:

```text
Monday–Saturday
10:00–22:00
```

and the configured competition timezone.

The system should then determine:

```text
Earliest possible completion
```

and:

```text
Latest allowable completion
```

for the Phase.

---

# 27. Phase Deadline

Every Phase must have a calculated deadline:

```text
phase_deadline
```

For normal phases:

```text
phase_deadline
≤
next phase's required start
```

For the final Phase:

```text
phase_deadline
≤
Season End Date
```

The final Phase additionally receives the protected final-week allocation.

---

# 28. Phase Planning Model

The competition engine should maintain:

```text
Current Phase
Remaining Phases
Available Season Time
Required Series
Available Scheduling Capacity
```

before scheduling.

Conceptually:

```text
Season
│
├── Current Date
│
├── Season End
│
├── Final Week Boundary
│
├── Current Phase
│
└── Estimated Remaining Competition
```

This becomes the basis for schedule feasibility.

---

# 29. Dynamic Phase Duration

The system should not assign:

```text
Phase 1 = 4 weeks
Phase 2 = 2 weeks
Phase 3 = 1 week
```

universally.

Instead it calculates the required duration from the actual competition.

For example:

```text
1000 players
→ many Phase 1 Series
→ greater scheduling requirement

500 survivors
→ fewer Phase 2 Series

250 survivors
→ fewer Phase 3 Series

...
→ final phase
→ very small number of Series
```

Therefore the Phase duration naturally decreases as the competition progresses.

---

# 30. Season Feasibility Check

Before competition scheduling begins, the system should calculate whether the Season configuration is feasible.

Conceptually:

```text
Season Start
+
Available Scheduling Capacity
+
Expected Competition Work
≤
Season End
```

If the competition cannot fit:

```text
Season Configuration
→ INVALID / INSUFFICIENT TIME
```

The Administrator must be informed before the competition is activated.

---

# 31. Registration Capacity and Season Lifetime

Participant capacity cannot be evaluated independently of time.

For example:

```text
100 players
```

may be feasible for a 12-week Season.

But:

```text
2,000 players
```

may require more scheduling capacity than the Season can provide.

Therefore the system must evaluate:

```text
Participant Count
+
Series Count
+
Schedule Capacity
+
Season Duration
```

together.

This is particularly important because the competition model intentionally supports large registration pools.

---

# 32. Season Creation Validation

When an Administrator creates a Season, the system should validate:

### Required

```text
Start Date exists
End Date exists
Start < End
Timezone exists
```

### Competition timing

```text
Season provides sufficient competition time
Final week is available
Allowed scheduling days exist
Allowed scheduling hours are valid
```

### Registration timing

```text
Registration Open
<
Registration Close
≤
Season Start / competition lock boundary
```

The exact registration relationship should be finalized in the Season lifecycle implementation ticket.

# 33. Season Lifecycle

The overall lifecycle becomes:

```text
DRAFT
   ↓
REGISTRATION_OPEN
   ↓
REGISTRATION_CLOSED
   ↓
ROSTER_LOCKED
   ↓
COMPETITION_INITIALIZATION
   ↓
PHASE_1_ACTIVE
   ↓
PHASE_1_COMPLETED
   ↓
PHASE_2_ACTIVE
   ↓
PHASE_2_COMPLETED
   ↓
...
   ↓
FINAL_PHASE_ACTIVE
   ↓
FINAL_PHASE_COMPLETED
   ↓
SEASON_COMPLETED
   ↓
ARCHIVED
```

The exact persisted Season status vocabulary may continue using the existing implementation where appropriate, but Phase activity must now exist independently inside the Season.

---

# 34. Season Completion

A Season cannot be completed merely because its calendar end date has arrived.

The competition engine should require:

```text
Final Phase completed
+
Final Series resolved
+
Champion/outcome determined
+
Required results finalized
```

before marking the Season:

```text
COMPLETED
```

However, the competition must be planned so that this completion occurs before the Season End Date.

---

# 35. Final Week Protection

The final week is therefore a **deadline reservation**.

The system must avoid scheduling earlier phases so late that:

```text
Final Phase
cannot fit inside final week
```

If this situation occurs:

```text
Schedule Generation
→ BLOCKED
```

with a clear reason such as:

> The remaining Season lifetime cannot accommodate the required final Phase within the protected final week.

---

# 36. Operator Adjustment

Administrators may manually adjust Series schedules before schedule lock.

However, manual adjustment must still respect:

```text
Season Start
Season End
Final Phase Deadline
Monday–Saturday
10:00–22:00
1-hour Series Match Window
Check-in Window
Results Window
```

An operator cannot manually create an appointment that the automatic scheduler would reject.

---

# 37. Schedule Validation

Schedule validation must therefore check:

```text
Series has appointment
        ↓
Appointment is inside Season
        ↓
Day is Monday–Saturday
        ↓
Match Window is 10:00–22:00
        ↓
Match Window = 1 hour
        ↓
Check-in window is valid
        ↓
Results deadline is valid
        ↓
No player conflict
        ↓
No Series conflict
        ↓
Phase deadline satisfied
        ↓
Final Phase deadline satisfied
```

This becomes part of the canonical Schedule Validation Engine.

---

# 38. No Sunday Auto-Scheduling

The automatic scheduler must never generate:

```text
Sunday Series
```

even if Sunday appears to provide otherwise useful capacity.

Sunday may remain an administrative/non-competition day.

If a future rule permits Sunday competition, that must be an explicit competition configuration change rather than an implicit scheduler behavior.

---

# 39. No Overnight Series

The scheduler must never create a Series that crosses the daily operating boundary.

Invalid:

```text
21:30–22:30
```

Valid:

```text
21:00–22:00
```

This rule prevents a one-hour Series from extending beyond the allowed competition hours.

---

# 40. No Unrealistic Compression

The scheduler must not solve a deadline problem by generating an unrealistic density of Series.

For example, if a Phase contains more Series than can reasonably be scheduled within:

```text
Monday–Saturday
10:00–22:00
```

the system must report:

```text
INSUFFICIENT_SCHEDULING_CAPACITY
```

rather than simply stacking impossible appointments.

---

# 41. Competition Timing Principle

The complete scheduling principle is:

```text
Season Lifetime
        ↓
Final Week Reservation
        ↓
Remaining Phase Time
        ↓
Phase Series Count
        ↓
Available Scheduling Capacity
        ↓
Series Appointments
```

The scheduler works **from the Season boundary downward**.

It must not work only from individual Series upward.

---

# 42. Example — 12-Week Season

Suppose:

```text
Season:
12 weeks

Start:
Week 1

End:
Week 12
```

The system knows:

```text
Week 12
=
Final Phase reservation
```

It then evaluates the expected competition:

```text
Phase 1
Largest field

Phase 2
Smaller field

Phase 3
Smaller field

Final Phase
Smallest field
```

The scheduler calculates whether Phase 1 can fit while preserving enough capacity for:

```text
Phase 2
+
Phase 3
+
Final Phase
```

If yes:

```text
Schedule generation allowed
```

If no:

```text
Schedule generation blocked
```

---

# 43. Example — Large Registration

Suppose:

```text
1,000 players
12-week Season
```

The competition engine may produce approximately:

```text
Phase 1
1,000 players
↓
500 potential winners

Phase 2
500 players
↓
250 potential winners

Phase 3
250 players
↓
125 potential winners

...
```

The actual field can decrease faster because a Series can eliminate both players under the draw/unresolved rules.

The scheduler must therefore use the **actual current Phase Series count**, not assume that every Phase will produce exactly half the participants.

---

# 44. Relationship to Phase Generation

Phase generation and scheduling are related but separate responsibilities.

### Phase Engine determines:

```text
Who competes
How many plots
Who belongs to each plot
Which Series exist
```

### Scheduling Engine determines:

```text
When each Series is played
```

### Season Timing Engine determines:

```text
Whether the entire competition can fit
```

Therefore:

```text
Phase Engine
      ↓
Series Engine
      ↓
Season Timing Engine
      ↓
Scheduling Engine
```

# 49. Canonical Timing Rules

The current canonical rules are:

```text
1. Every Season has a Start Date.

2. Every Season has an End Date.

3. The Season lifetime is the hard competition boundary.

4. All Phases must fit inside the Season lifetime.

5. Phase duration is dynamically determined by competition workload and available scheduling capacity.

6. Phase 1 normally requires the most time because it contains the largest field.

7. Later Phases normally require less time as the field decreases.

8. The final Phase must be completed within the final week of the Season.

9. Automatically generated Series may only be scheduled Monday–Saturday.

10. Sunday is not an automatic competition scheduling day.

11. Series Match Windows may only begin and end within the 10:00–22:00 operating period.

12. Every Series has a one-hour Match Window.

13. All three Games in a Series must occur inside that one-hour window.

14. Check-in opens one hour before the Series.

15. Check-in closes 30 minutes before the Series.

16. The Results Window closes 30 minutes after the Match Window.

17. The scheduler must account for the Season deadline when generating Phase schedules.

18. The scheduler must reject schedules that cannot fit inside the Season lifetime.

19. Manual operator adjustments must obey the same timing constraints.

20. The system must never solve insufficient capacity by generating unrealistic schedules.
```

# 50. Final Architecture

The resulting temporal architecture is:

```text
                    SEASON
        ┌───────────────────────────┐
        │ Start Date                │
        │ End Date                  │
        │ Timezone                  │
        │                           │
        │ Final Week Reservation    │
        └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                      │
                      ↓
              COMPETITION PLAN
                      │
          ┌───────────┴───────────┐
          ↓                       ↓
       PHASE 1                 PHASE 2
       largest                 smaller
          │                       │
       PLOTS                    PLOTS
          │                       │
       SERIES                   SERIES
          │                       │
       GAMES                    GAMES
          │                       │
          └───────────┬───────────┘
                      ↓
                FINAL PHASE
                      │
                      ↓
              FINAL WEEK
                      │
                      ↓
             SEASON COMPLETED
```

The fundamental relationship is:

> **The Season defines the available time. The competition engine divides that time among Phases. The scheduling engine fills each Phase with realistic Series appointments.**

# 52. Implementation Consequence

The replacement competition engine must therefore be designed around:

```text
Season
    ↓
Season Timing
    ↓
Phase Planning
    ↓
Plot Distribution
    ↓
Series Generation
    ↓
Series Scheduling
    ↓
Game Execution
    ↓
Series Resolution
    ↓
Advancement
    ↓
Next Phase
    ↓
Final Phase
    ↓
Season Completion
```

The old assumption:

```text
Generate all Fixtures
→ Generate Schedule
```

is no longer valid.

The system must instead progressively generate and schedule the competition **Phase by Phase**, while continuously checking the remaining Season lifetime.
